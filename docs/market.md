# Market and validation

**Verdict:** there is a market category, not an empty category. Building "AI mock interviews + resumes + a job tracker" is not differentiation. The testable opportunity is **whether an inspectable record of real experience produces better second attempts and more trustworthy application drafts with less rework**.

### Competitive landscape

Primary product pages and US list prices reviewed **25 November 2026**. These are vendor descriptions, not hands-on benchmarks or evidence that a competitor lacks a feature. Billing intervals and included practice differ.

### Who buys, and why now in their search?

The initial buyer is an individual with a round already scheduled, several projects worth discussing, and little time to reconstruct them. The trigger is the calendar invitation; the first win is an overlooked example and a stronger retry that evening. A new graduate with little experience, an active-interview answer assistant, and a recruiter screening candidates are three different products; don't optimize for them in the pilot.

Acquire the first cohort through opt-in engineering alumni groups, interview coaches and career communities. Offer one free mock with a real debrief, not an email gate on a generic question list. Test a coach referral only with explicit candidate sharing; never give the referrer transcript access by default. After product pull, test other role families with their own calibrated rubrics, then outplacement/coaching partnerships. Avoid paid broad-intent "AI resume" traffic until payback is known.

**What might compound:** a candidate's own curated evidence becomes more useful as they refine it; a library of *synthetic or separately licensed* rubric calibration examples improves the coach. Neither requires pooling personal resumes. **What won't defend the product:** a prompt, access to the same model API, a scraped question bank, or locking users out of exports. Incumbents can copy visible features. Trust, calibration and repeat use have to be earned.

### Pricing, reachable demand and reasons to stop

Test the [Free and Prep bundles](operations.md#billing-and-unit-economics) against a story-bank subscription and a free general assistant. Use interviews about willingness to pay and measured delivery costs to select a pilot price; none has been set. Easy cancellation matters: successful customers may leave when they land a job. Track paid months per search season rather than indefinite retention.

An illustrative **bottom-up channel model**, not a forecast: 1,000 qualified visits/month x 20% completing a first mock x 15% of completers paying = 30 new payers/month. At two paid months per search, steady state is about 60 subscribers. Monthly revenue would be `60 x the chosen monthly price` before costs. Measure acquisition per *paying* user, including referral fees.

| Experiment | Proposed pass/fail gate | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concierge pilot: 20 candidates bring one real JD; counterbalance product vs chatbot on matched questions. Two blinded coaches judge retries; disclose small-sample uncertainty. | At least 12/20 prefer the evidence-backed debrief and reviewers prefer its retry in >=60% of non-tied comparisons. Track intake time and unsupported claims separately. | If not, improve elicitation/rubric or narrow the role; don't scale acquisition. |
| In 12 paired rehearsals, compare the live guide with paper notes containing the same projects and outcomes. Counterbalance order; agree on intended points beforehand. | At least 8/12 candidates cover one additional intended point with no increase in self-reported distraction. No observation or recording of real interviews required. | If not, keep the simple manual deck; don't invest in adaptive listening. |
| First 100 activated candidates, voluntary privacy-preserving funnel | Median time to first useful debrief <25 minutes; >=35% complete a second practice within 7 days. Ask non-returners whether they finished their search. | If people only export resumes, revisit the interview-first thesis. |
| Offer the real monthly price after a useful free session | >=10% of activated users pay within 14 days; target paid acquisition payback within one paid month. Count refunds and abandoned checkouts. | If value is episodic but real, test the short pass before lowering price. |
| Pilot a clearly bounded job feed with 20 opted-in candidates | >=50% of weekly digests lead to at least one save, draft review or practice; track stale postings and "not for me" reasons. | Narrow boards/roles or defer discovery if coverage is poor. Never compensate with unauthorized scraping. |

Offer/interview conversion can be an **optional self-report**. It is lagged, selective and confounded; it is not the training label for hire/no-hire and not a marketing claim.
